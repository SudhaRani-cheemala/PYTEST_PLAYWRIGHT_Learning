1. First understand the problem

Imagine you are on a website:

Main Tab
   |
   | Click "Terms & Conditions"
   ↓
New Tab opens

So now you have:

Tab 1 → Main website
Tab 2 → Terms & Conditions

In Selenium, you normally hear about window handles and switch_to.window().

In Playwright, it is much simpler:

Every tab is a Page object.

That's the most important thing to remember.


2. What is a Page?

When you write:

page = context.new_page()

you are creating one browser tab.

Think:

Browser
   ↓
Context
   ↓
Page

For example:

page = context.new_page()

means:

Page = Tab 1


3. What happens when a second tab opens?

Suppose Tab 1 has a button:

[ Open New Tab ]

When you click it:

Tab 1                    Tab 2
------                   ------
Main website             New website

Playwright creates another Page object.

So:

page      → Tab 1
new_page  → Tab 2

4. How do we capture the new tab?

This is the important syntax:

with context.expect_page() as page_info:
    page.click("button")

new_page = page_info.value

Step 1
with context.expect_page() as page_info:

You are telling Playwright:

"I am about to do something that should open a new tab. Please wait and capture that new tab."

Step 2
page.click("button")

You click the button.

Step 3

The new tab opens.

Step 4
new_page = page_info.value


Now new_page contains the new tab.

So:

page
 ↓
Tab 1

new_page
 ↓
Tab 2
